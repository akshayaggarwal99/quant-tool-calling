4                                                 Abstract

5           Background. Language models run on laptops after post-training quantization, and the
6      format that ships is the llama.cpp k-quant. The accuracy cost of that compression has been
7      measured on prose, knowledge and reasoning benchmarks, and not on the output an agent
8      produces, which is a function call that a program then executes.
9           Methods. We built a ﬁve-rung k-quant ladder, Q8_0 to Q3_K_M, from one FP16 source
10     for Qwen3-1.7B, and three-rung ladders for Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Llama-3.2-3B and
11     Llama-3.1-8B. Every rung was scored on the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard’s
12     simple_python category by deterministic syntax-tree matching (n = 400 or 200), with a
13     type-coerced secondary score, and on 400 GSM8K questions with the same weight ﬁles.
14     Rungs are compared with McNemar’s exact test on identical items, Holm-corrected within
15     each model; every accuracy and paired difference carries a 95% interval; two rungs were run
16     three times to measure the instrument. Five hypotheses with thresholds were written in a
17     dated ﬁle on the ﬁrst day; its ﬁrst commit postdates the ﬁrst ladder, so the study is not called
18     pre-registered, and every departure from the ﬁle is tabulated.
19          Results. On Qwen3-1.7B tool calling is ﬂat from Q8_0 through Q4_K_M and loses 4.75
20     points at Q3_K_M (Holm-adjusted p = 0.017), about 10 times the run-to-run range.
21     Free-form mathematics on those ﬁles loses 39.50 points at that rung because 213 of 400
22     generations reach the 4,096-token budget while still reasoning; 29 of them commit to a
23     correct answer ﬁrst. Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B show no loss at any rung. Llama-3.2-3B
24     loses 5.50 strict points at Q3_K_M (Holm p = 0.030) and a suggestive 4.00 at Q4_K_M
25     (p = 0.021, Holm 0.064, one run); under the coerced verdict neither loss is detectable.
26     Llama-3.1-8B’s strict score rises from 50.00% to 71.50% down the ladder because its
27     full-precision weights write numbers as quoted strings; scored on the parsed value, with 13 of
28     its 14 three-bit decode failures re-read as the well-formed JSON calls they are, it scores
29     93.00%, 93.00% and 91.50%. A logit penalty on overthinking markers changes tool-calling
30     accuracy by 0.00 points. The multi-turn arm is confounded: 46, 59 and 137 of 200 trajectories
31     aborted on a server context limit shared by four slots.
32          Conclusions. At four bits no model shows a single-call loss larger than its paired interval
33     allows: under the coerced verdict no lower interval end is below −5.2 points and the
34     minimum detectable difference is 2.8 to 3.7 points. At three bits Qwen3-1.7B and, under strict
35     scoring, Llama-3.2-3B lose accuracy; the other three models do not detectably. The
36     length-driven collapse of chain-of-thought reasoning under three-bit weights does not reach a
37     single tool call. A strict schema score can move in either direction under quantization because
38     it also measures a formatting habit, and part of a checker’s decode-failure count can be its
39     own parser, so a coerced score and the failure types belong beside it.

42   1    Introduction
43   A laptop runs Qwen3-1.7B in its three-bit k-quant, Q3_K_M, as the model behind a small agent.
44   The agent is asked to ﬁnd the greatest common divisor of 36 and 48 and is handed one function to
45   call, number_theory.gcd. The same model at eight bits, Q8_0, answers with the call
46   number_theory.gcd(number1=36, number2=48). At three bits it answers with a sentence:
47   “The Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) of two numbers, say 36 and 48, is 24.” The program
48   waiting for a call receives prose, and the number in the prose is wrong as well; the answer is 12.
49   Both outputs came from one FP16 weight ﬁle. Only the number of bits used to store each weight
50   changed. This is item simple_python_21 of the benchmark used below, one of 30 items the
51   three-bit weights lose against the eight-bit weights, and the question of this paper is how often that
52   happens, at which bit-width it starts, and whether the function call or the free-form reasoning
53   breaks ﬁrst.
54   The compression is post-training quantization (Dettmers et al., 2022; Frantar et al., 2023; Lin
55   et al., 2024). The full-precision ﬁle for Qwen3-1.7B is 4.1 GB; the version most people would run,
56   Q4_K_M, is 1.3 GB, about 32% of it. The format nearly everyone uses on a laptop is the family of
57   llama.cpp k-quants (Gerganov and contributors, 2023), because that is what Ollama and the
58   desktop front ends serve, and a systematic review of language-model deployment on edge devices
59   counts model compression, quantization included, as a central enabler of running these models on
60   memory-limited hardware (Dinçer and Kilimci, 2026). Four bits is where the scaling analysis says
61   the trade-off is best (Dettmers and Zettlemoyer, 2023; Kumar et al., 2025), and it is the default
62   most front ends pull. Lower bit-widths are pursued for throughput as well as size, although
63   Malekar and Zand (2025) show, for binary and ternary weights, that the throughput gain is capped
64   by whatever part of the model stays in higher precision.
65   The cost of that compression has been measured with care, on tasks other than the one an agent
66   performs. Broad evaluations cover language modelling, classiﬁcation, knowledge, dialogue, long
67   context and trustworthiness across many families (Jin et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024); Kurtic et al.
68   (2025) score the Llama-3.1 family under FP8, INT8 and INT4 on academic benchmarks and on
69   chat, code-generation and long-context tasks; Lee et al. (2025) score instruction-tuned models
70   from 1B to 405B under four quantization methods on thirteen datasets; multilingual degradation
71   has its own study (Marchisio et al., 2024); long-context inputs lose up to 59% under four-bit
72   methods (Mekala et al., 2025); reasoning models have been quantized and scored on mathematics
73   and code (Liu et al., 2025). Kurt (2026) quantizes Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct into thirteen GGUF
74   formats and scores each on GSM8K, HellaSwag, IFEval, MMLU, TruthfulQA and perplexity.
75   Lotﬁ et al. (2026) measure ﬁve reasoning models under GPTQ, AWQ and FlatQuant on
76   mathematics, code and science questions and ﬁnd accuracy falling as chain-of-thought inﬂates.
77   Every task in every one of these papers is free-form or multiple choice. None involves emitting a
78   function call.
79   An agent calls tools (Patil et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2025). It emits a function name


                                                        2
80    and a set of typed arguments that a program will execute. That output either passes the checker or
81    it does not, with no partial credit and no reader to accept an approximate answer. Our starting
82    intuition was that a function signature has none of the slack prose has, so compression should
83    break tool calling at a higher bit-width than it breaks free-form accuracy. We wrote this down as
84    our ﬁrst hypothesis, with a threshold, in the hypothesis ﬁle described in §3.1.
85    We built a controlled ladder, ﬁve k-quant formats produced from one FP16 source, and scored
86    each rung on the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (Patil et al., 2025), which compares a
87    model’s call to a reference by deterministic abstract-syntax-tree matching; no model judges
88    another model anywhere in the pipeline. We then ran each rung’s ﬁle on a free-form control so
89    that the two ﬂoors could be compared on identical parameters, repeated Q8_0, Q4_K_M and
90    Q3_K_M on four further models in two families, and re-scored every run under a checker that
91    accepts a correctly valued argument in the wrong type.
92    For Qwen3-1.7B the answer is the reverse of the one we expected. Tool-calling accuracy is ﬂat
93    from Q8_0 through Q4_K_M and falls by 4.75 points at Q3_K_M, roughly 10 times the
94    run-to-run noise of the instrument. On those ﬁles free-form mathematics falls by 39.50 points at
95    the same rung. Both ﬂoors sit between four and three bits, but the drop in tool calling is about an
96    eighth of the drop in mathematics. The difference comes down to output length. At three bits the
97    model’s chain of thought (Wei et al., 2022) grows until 53% of math generations use up their
98    token budget before reaching an answer. A tool call is a median of 155 tokens at that rung, so the
99    weights never get the room to wander. On Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B there is no cliff at any
100   rung. On the Llama family the picture is different, and part of the difference is the checker rather
101   than the weights: Llama-3.2-3B loses at three bits under strict scoring and not under coerced
102   scoring, and Llama-3.1-8B’s strict score rises under compression because its full-precision
103   weights quote numbers, a habit the compressed weights partly lose (§4.5).

133   2     Related work
134   Quantization evaluated on free-form tasks. The broad evaluations of post-training quantization
135   score many model families on language modelling, multiple-choice knowledge, dialogue,
136   long-context and trustworthiness tasks (Jin et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024); a full-text search of both
137   ﬁnds no tool-calling or function-calling benchmark. Kurtic et al. (2025) run more than half a
138   million evaluations on the Llama-3.1 family under FP8, INT8 and INT4 and ﬁnd FP8 effectively
139   lossless, well-tuned INT8 1 to 3% below the BF16 baseline, and INT4 weight-only quantization
140   on par with 8-bit; Lee et al. (2025) ﬁnd quantized models generally ahead of smaller full-precision
141   ones but weaker on instruction following and hallucination detection. Neither scores a function
142   call. Liu et al. (2025) quantize the DeepSeek-R1-distilled and QwQ reasoning models and ﬁnd
143   mathematics and code degrading under aggressive weight and KV-cache quantization, again on
144   free-form tasks. Kurt (2026) is the closest work and the one this paper extends: one model,
145   thirteen llama.cpp formats from Q3_K_S to Q8_0 against FP16, six free-form or multiple-choice
146   benchmarks, plus throughput and size. A full-text read ﬁnds no tool-calling, function-calling,
147   agentic, structured-output or schema-compliance evaluation, and its conclusion asks for exactly
148   what we add, additional model families and sizes under the same protocol. Lotﬁ et al. (2026) take
149   a different axis, in the line of work on overthinking in reasoning models (Chen et al., 2024; Sui
150   et al., 2025). Under GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023), AWQ (Lin et al., 2024) and FlatQuant, ﬁve
151   reasoning models lose accuracy while emitting longer chains of thought, and in up to 52% of
152   failures the correct answer appears mid-trace without being committed to. Their compression
153   methods are research methods rather than the k-quants that ship to laptops, and their failure
154   categorisation, including the overthinking class, relies on GPT-5 as a judge. Setting quantization
155   aside, the gain from zero-shot chain-of-thought prompting over direct answering holds on average
156   across six models and six question-answering datasets but varies in size by model and dataset
157   (Hebenstreit et al., 2024); what quantization does to the length of the chain is a further axis.
158   Surveys of language models for constrained deployment list quantization among the standard
159   compression techniques (Turkmen, 2026; Dinçer and Kilimci, 2026), and the survey of small
160   clinical models names agentic tool use as a future direction (Turkmen, 2026); neither measures the


                                                        4
161   two together.
162   Tool-calling evaluation. Gorilla (Patil et al., 2024) and ToolLLM (Qin et al., 2024) established
163   API calling as a benchmark task, and τ -bench (Yao et al., 2025) extends it to multi-turn agent-user
164   interaction. BFCL (Patil et al., 2025) scores a model’s function call against a reference by
165   abstract-syntax-tree matching over the function name, argument names, types and values, across
166   single, multiple and parallel calls, an irrelevance category where no call is correct, and multi-turn
167   trajectories. Because the matcher is deterministic, the same generation always receives the same
168   verdict, so a difference between two rungs can be attributed to the weights rather than to the
169   scorer. Dondeti (2026) study the same class of small models on consumer hardware from the
170   safety side, scoring adversarial tool calls from seven models between 1B and 4B parameters with
171   a deterministic would-dispatch rule; this paper is the accuracy side of the same deployment.
172   The gap. Quantization has been scored on prose, knowledge, instruction following and reasoning,
173   for research compression methods and for the deployed one. It has not been scored on tool calling
174   under either. That is what we measure.

189   3.2 Problem statement
190   A ladder for a model M is an ordered list of weight ﬁles r0 , r1 , . . . , rK produced from one FP16
191   source with one quantizer, in decreasing bits per weight, so that the ﬁles differ in their weights and
192   in nothing else. Here r0 is always Q8_0. An item set I is a ﬁxed list of n benchmark items with
193   reference answers. A verdict v(i, r) ∈ {0, 1} is the deterministic output of a checker on the model’s
194   response to item i served from rung r, and accuracy A(r) is the mean verdict over I. Two verdict
195   functions are used for tool calling. The strict verdict is BFCL’s abstract-syntax-tree match. The
196   coerced verdict equals the strict verdict except that a strict failure is re-checked by the same

197   checker after every string-typed argument whose schema declares a non-string type is replaced by
198   its parsed value when it parses as that type, and after an output the handler could not decode is
199   re-read as JSON when it is one well-formed call (§3.5); a quoted number, list or boolean counts
200   when the parsed value is what the reference holds and not otherwise. The coerced verdict is never
201   below the strict one.
202   Rungs are compared to r0 pairwise on the same items. The test is McNemar’s exact test on the
203   discordant pairs (McNemar, 1947): lost counts items with v(i, r0 ) = 1 and v(i, r) = 0, gained the
204   reverse, and p is the two-sided exact binomial probability of a split at least that uneven under the
205   null of no difference. Tests are grouped into families and corrected within each family by Holm’s
206   step-down method (Holm, 1979). A family is every distinct McNemar test against one model’s
207   Q8_0 on the single-call arm, under both verdicts; for the Qwen3 models the coerced verdicts equal
208   the strict ones, so the family has one test per rung (4 for Qwen3-1.7B, 2 each for Qwen3-8B and
209   Qwen3-14B), and for the Llama models two per rung (4 each). The free-form arm of each model,
210   the multi-turn arm and the penalty arm are families of their own. The paper reports 28 tests in 10
211   families, and every table gives the raw p with the Holm-adjusted p in brackets. Every accuracy
212   carries a Wilson score 95% interval, in square brackets, and every paired difference carries the
213   adjusted Wald 95% interval of Agresti and Min (2005). The cliff rung c(M) is the
214   highest-precision rung rk , k ≥ 1, with A(rk ) < A(r0 ) and Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 against r0 . The
215   ﬂoor is the rung immediately above the cliff rung, the lowest-bit rung on the ladder with no
216   detected loss. A model is ﬂat on the ladder when it has no cliff rung, so its ﬂoor is at or below the
217   lowest rung measured. A detected loss must also exceed the instrument tolerance τ , the largest
218   range in accuracy across three runs of identical weights and settings (§4.1); on this instrument
219   τ = 0.50 points, and every loss reported as a cliff below is at least 10 times larger. The test, the
220   interval, α and the cliff rule were chosen on the evening of 18 September 2026, after the ﬁrst
221   ladder had run and not in the hypothesis ﬁle; the Holm correction and the paired intervals were
222   added after review.
223   “No detectable loss” has a size. For each model at Q4_K_M, Table 6 gives the minimum
224   detectable difference: the smallest paired loss McNemar’s test would detect at α = 0.05 with 80%
225   power at the discordance rate observed on that model, from the sample-size formula of Connor
226   (1987) solved for the difference. At n = 200 and the discordance seen here it is 2.8 to 3.7 points
227   under the coerced verdict, so a loss of three points on an n = 200 model is inside what this design
228   cannot exclude, and every “no detectable loss” below should be read with that bound beside it.
229   Item counts are n = 400 for the Qwen3-1.7B ladder and both free-form arms, and n = 200 for the
230   other four models and for the multi-turn category, which has 200 items in all. Every rung is a
231   single run except the two Qwen3-1.7B rungs used for the tolerance measurement, so a stranger
232   who repeats one rung should expect its accuracy to move within τ and its verdicts on a few items
233   to ﬂip.
234   Under these deﬁnitions the cliff rung is Q3_K_M for Qwen3-1.7B under both verdicts, none for
235   Qwen3-8B and none for Qwen3-14B (both ﬂat), Q3_K_M strict and none coerced for
236   Llama-3.2-3B, and none for Llama-3.1-8B under either verdict. The Results section gives the
237   numbers behind each.

238   3.3 The ladder
239   Every rung of Qwen3-1.7B (Qwen Team, 2025) is produced from a single FP16 GGUF with
240   llama-quantize. The formats are a subset of the ladder in Kurt (2026), chosen to span the range
241   at one rung per bit-width: Q8_0, Q6_K, Q5_K_M, Q4_K_M and Q3_K_M. Ollama’s library
242   publishes only two of these ﬁve for this model, Q8_0 and Q4_K_M, alongside FP16, which is
243   why the ladder is built rather than pulled. A Qwen3-4B rung was planned and dropped:
244   bfcl-eval has no entry for the original Qwen3-4B, only for the later Instruct-2507 lineage, and
245   mixing lineages would have confounded the size comparison. Four further models get three rungs,
246   Q8_0, Q4_K_M and Q3_K_M: the top of the ladder, the default most front ends pull, and the ﬁrst
247   rung below it. Each is built from its own FP16 source with the same quantizer: Qwen3-8B and
248   Qwen3-14B from the same family, and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct from a
249   second family, whose Llama 3.1 models are described in Llama Team, AI @ Meta (2024). The
250   FP16 sources are the *-fp16 blobs Ollama distributes. Per model the design is: Qwen3-1.7B, 5
251   rungs, n = 400, with 3 runs at Q8_0 and Q4_K_M; Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Llama-3.2-3B and
252   Llama-3.1-8B, 3 rungs each, n = 200, 1 run per rung. Both Llama models are instruction-tuned
253   rather than reasoning-tuned, which is deliberate: it separates what the ladder does to a tool call
254   from what it does to a chain of thought. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is also the model Kurt (2026)
255   quantized, so the two ladders meet on one model.


256   3.4 Serving
257   Each rung is served by llama-server from llama.cpp (the binary reports version 0.4.1, build
258   10964, commit b29c606e2; Homebrew) with full Metal ofﬂoad on an Apple M1 Max with 64 GB
259   of memory, under a ﬁxed model alias, so the harness sends identical requests to every rung. The
260   single-call ladder and the repeats use 4 slots and an 8,192-token context; the free-form control, the
261   larger models, the Llama models and the multi-turn runs use 32,768, because reasoning traces
262   overﬂowed the smaller context; the 16,384-token budget re-run uses 65,536 with two slots. In
263   every conﬁguration the KV cache is one buffer shared by the slots (kv_unified), so concurrent
264   requests share the context ﬁgure rather than each owning it. A single call has a median prompt of
265   252 tokens and a median completion of 155 to 195 tokens (§4.3), so the context setting cannot
266   affect the single-call arms, and the cross-model comparison in Table 5 is unaffected by the two
267   settings it mixes. It does affect the multi-turn arm, where trajectories accumulate a median context
268   of 6,591 to 6,879 tokens per request and four of them together overﬂow the shared cache (§4.6).
269   Generation is at temperature 0.001, the bfcl-eval default, for both arms. No sampling seed is
270   ﬁxed: llama-server is run with its default seed of −1, which draws a fresh random seed per
271   request, so decoding is near-greedy but not bit-identical between runs. The repeat control below
272   measures what that leaves. For the tool-calling arm, bfcl-eval 2026.3.23 builds the prompt itself
273   in each model’s own function-calling format and sends it to the server’s raw completions endpoint,
274   so the server’s --jinja chat template is not involved in those runs; the free-form arm uses the
275   chat completions endpoint, where the template applies. Throughput, recorded in the lab notebook
276   rather than computed from the logs, is about 35 tokens per second per slot and 650 tokens per
277   second of prompt evaluation; the median latency of a single call on the Qwen3-1.7B ladder, from
278   the result ﬁles, is 4.5 to 6.7 s. Python is 3.12.

                                                       8
279   3.5 Scoring
280   BFCL v4 (Patil et al., 2025), simple_python category, deterministic AST matching, run through
281   the -FC handler of each model so that the model uses its native function-calling format. The strict
282   verdict is the checker’s own. The coerced verdict is computed afterwards from the score ﬁles, with
283   no new generation: each strict failure is passed back through BFCL’s
284   simple_function_checker with string arguments parsed to the type the function schema
285   declares (analysis/coerced.py in the repository). A quoted integer is parsed only if it is an
286   integer literal, a quoted list only if it parses as a list, a quoted boolean only if it is true or false;
287   anything else is left as the string it was, and the checker then compares the parsed value with the
288   reference exactly as it would a natively typed one, including nested types and optional parameters.
289   One further step was added in this revision after reading the raw outputs behind the Llama-3.1-8B
290   decode failures (§4.5): bfcl-eval’s Llama 3.1 handler decodes a single call with Python’s
291   eval(), which refuses the JSON literals true, false and null that Meta’s tool-call format
292   allows. A strict decode failure whose raw output json.loads() reads as exactly one {"name":
293   ..., "parameters": {...}} object is treated as that call and checked like any other;
294   anything else stays a decode failure. The Qwen3 runs are unchanged by either step: 0 of their 226
295   strict failures across all rungs are of the coercible kind and 0 are decode failures. Differences
296   between rungs are tested with McNemar’s exact test on the discordant pairs, corrected within
297   families, and every accuracy carries a Wilson 95% interval, as deﬁned in §3.2.


298   3.6 Reproducibility control
299   Two rungs of Qwen3-1.7B, Q8_0 and Q4_K_M, were run three times each with the same weights
300   and settings. This gives the run-to-run variation of the instrument, which is needed before any
301   difference between rungs can be read as a difference between weights. The control was run on the
302   1.7B model only; the other four models are single runs and inherit its tolerance as an assumption,
303   which the Limitations section returns to.


304   3.7 Free-form control
305   The same ﬁve Qwen3-1.7B weight ﬁles, and the three ﬁles of each Llama model, answer 400
306   GSM8K test questions (Cobbe et al., 2021), drawn at seed 42, through the same server under the
307   same alias, with a 4,096-token completion budget. Every generation is logged in full so that
308   scoring under a different extractor is a re-read rather than a re-run. Two extractors are used. Strict
309   accepts an explicit commitment in any of three forms, “The answer is N”, “#### N”, or
310   \boxed{N}, and takes the last such commitment in the text, so a boxed number mid-trace
311   followed by a later “The answer is” yields the later one. Lenient takes the last number in the
312   visible answer. Our ﬁrst strict rule accepted only the ﬁrst form; on the ﬁrst 59 Q8_0 generations it
313   would have scored 21 wrong for writing the answer in a box. That rule was a strawman, it was
314   replaced before any rung was scored (commit 07cee6b, 18 September, 17:56), and the gap it
315   would have produced is not reported anywhere in this paper.

338   3.9 Overthinking penalty
339   Lotﬁ et al. (2026) report that a ﬁxed logit penalty on a curated list of overthinking markers cuts

343   method with a single ﬁxed λ rather than their tuned value.

364   4.2 Flat to four bits, a cliff at three
365   Table 3 is the ladder. From Q8_0 down to Q4_K_M the accuracy sits in a band of under three

380   Figure 1 puts both tasks on one axis and Table 4 gives the numbers. The tool-calling line barely

383   else: no rung above Q3_K_M differs from Q8_0 under paired testing. So H1 fails, and it fails in
384   the opposite direction. At Q3_K_M free-form accuracy falls by 39.50 points, against 4.75 for tool
385   calling.
386   The mechanism is in the last two columns of the table. Q8_0 already leaves 28 of 400 generations
387   (7%) at the 4,096-token budget, all of them without a committed answer. At Q3_K_M, 213 of 400
388   reach the budget while still inside the chain of thought: 164 contain no committed answer, 20
389   commit to a wrong one, and 29 commit to the right answer and keep reasoning past it, so they
390   count as correct. The 187 correct answers at this rung are 158 ﬁnished generations plus those 29.
391   The median completion length at this rung is the budget itself. This is the behaviour Lotﬁ et al.

507   4.7 Does the overthinking penalty transfer
508   The penalty has no measurable effect. With it, Qwen3-1.7B scores 92.00% at Q4_K_M and
509   87.00% at Q3_K_M, identical to the unpenalised runs to two decimals; at each rung 2 questions
510   ﬂip one way and 2 the other, within the repeat-control range of Table 2. H4 predicted a loss of at
511   least three points and there is no loss. The explanation follows from §4.3: the penalty acts on
512   tokens a reasoning trace emits and a tool call does not, so on a 155-token output there is almost
513   nothing for it to act on. This agrees with the authors’ own scope statement that the marker list was
514   curated for free-form English reasoning. Both the failure they describe and the ﬁx for it live in the
515   reasoning trace, and the tool call contains very little of that trace.

538   5      Discussion
539   Of the ﬁve hypotheses, one held as written, three did not, and one cannot be read from this run.
540   H1, that the schema would break ﬁrst, reversed under both its registered statistic and AST
541   accuracy. H2, that failures would turn structural, moved +6.3 points on the primary model against
542   a threshold of 15. H3, that the AST drop would be smaller than the strict free-form drop by at least
543   ﬁve points, held by 34.75 points; the separate question it was meant to bound, how much of a
544   free-form drop is the parser, has the answer 0.50 points at most. H4, that the overthinking penalty
545   would cost schema compliance, found a change of 0.00 points. H5, on multi-turn ampliﬁcation, is
546   confounded by a shared server cache and is reported as such rather than as the wide-margin
547   support earlier drafts claimed.
548   The tool-calling ﬂoor is a property of the model, not of the format. At four bits no model shows a


                                                            18
549   loss larger than the intervals in Table 6 allow, which is three to ﬁve points depending on the model
550   and the verdict, and the coerced verdict at Q4_K_M sits within 2.50 points of Q8_0 on every
551   model; three bits costs Qwen3-1.7B 4.75 points and Llama-3.2-3B 5.50 strict points, and costs
552   Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B and Llama-3.1-8B nothing detectable at these sample sizes. The
553   mechanism that damages free-form reasoning at three bits on Qwen3, a chain of thought that runs
554   past its budget, does not reach a single tool call because the call is over long before the run-away
555   starts.
556   On the measurement itself, three points for another group. First, report the failure types. The
557   Llama-3.1-8B inversion in the strict column of Table 5 would read as a broken harness to anyone
558   who did not see Table 7, and it took a re-score of the existing ﬁles, not a new run, to show that 86
559   of 100 failures were the right number in quotes. Second, read the raw outputs behind a checker’s
560   decode failures: 13 of 14 here were the checker’s parser, and a hypothesis about structural failure
561   (H2) would have been declared supported on that model without the reading. Third, measure the
562   instrument, and read its logs. Three runs with the same weights moved by at most 0.50 points
563   (Table 2); without that ﬁgure none of the other differences could be attributed to anything. The
564   multi-turn arm is the counter-example: a server error recorded in every result ﬁle went unread
565   through two drafts, and the arm’s headline number was the error rate.
566   Practical reading. For anyone deploying an agent on a laptop: four bits shows no single-call loss
567   larger than about three to ﬁve points on any model measured here, which is the bound the sample
568   sizes give and not a guarantee of zero; three bits costs Qwen3-1.7B and, under strict scoring,
569   Llama-3.2-3B; the bit-width should be measured on the model being shipped rather than assumed
570   from another family; a strict schema score should be read together with a coerced one and the
571   failure types; and the serving conﬁguration, not only the weights, decides whether a multi-turn
572   agent ﬁnishes, since the multi-turn arm here was decided by a shared cache before the weights had
573   a say. A model that emits malformed or wrong tool calls can take real actions, so agents should be
574   tested at the exact quantization and context conﬁguration they ship with.
575   Limitations. Three sizes of one model family and two sizes of a second, on the easiest BFCL
576   category plus one multi-turn set for Qwen3-1.7B only, on one hardware platform and one
577   inference server. The hypotheses were written in a ﬁle whose ﬁrst commit postdates the ﬁrst
578   ladder, so their priority rests on the author’s record. FP16 was the planned reference arm and was
579   not run; every comparison is against Q8_0. The ladder is one quantization family; GPTQ, AWQ
580   and the other research methods are deliberately out of scope, and multilingual degradation is
581   excluded because Marchisio et al. (2024) already cover it. The reproducibility control was run on
582   two rungs of one model rather than on every rung of every model, so the tolerance of 0.50 points
583   is measured on Qwen3-1.7B and assumed for the other four; the Llama-3.2-3B strict result at
584   Q4_K_M rests on 9 lost against 1 gained from a single run and does not survive the within-model
585   correction, and a repeat on that model is the second thing we would add. Four of the ﬁve models
586   were scored on the ﬁrst 200 items of the category, which halves their power relative to the 1.7B
587   ladder; the minimum detectable differences in Table 6 are the consequence. The free-form control
588   uses a 4,096-token budget; the 16,384-token re-run shows that a larger budget recovers few of the
589   213 truncated generations. The Llama-3.1-8B typing habit was diagnosed from the score ﬁles, the
590   raw outputs and the published prompting-mode number, not by re-running the weights on a
591   second server or through a second tool-template path; that cross-check is the ﬁrst thing we would


                                                       19
592   add. The overthinking penalty is one ﬁxed λ rather than the authors’ tuned value. The multi-turn
593   arm is confounded by a shared KV cache and has to be re-run with a per-trajectory context; until
594   then it says nothing about the weights. Latency was recorded for the 1.7B ladder only.


595   6    Conclusions
596   We expected a function signature, having no slack, to be the ﬁrst thing quantization breaks. On the
597   deployed format, scored deterministically, it was the output that held up best. For Qwen3-1.7B the
598   tool-calling ﬂoor and the free-form ﬂoor sit at the same rung, Q4_K_M, but the call loses 4.75
599   points at Q3_K_M where the mathematics loses 39.50, and the difference is length: three-bit
600   weights make the chain of thought grow past the token budget, and a call is complete long before
601   that happens. On Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B there is no tool-calling cliff at any rung. On the
602   Llama family the strict score moves in both directions because compression changes how the
603   models type their arguments, and part of what the checker reports as decode failure is its own
604   parser; scored on the parsed value, four bits costs neither Llama model detectable accuracy and
605   three bits costs Llama-3.1-8B 1.50 points, not detectable at n = 200. A fair extractor changes at
606   most 0.50 points, and a penalty designed for reasoning traces ﬁnds little to act on in a call. The
607   bit-width an agent can tolerate has to be measured on the model being shipped, with the bound the
608   sample size gives stated beside the result, and the damage quantization does to long reasoning
609   does not carry over to the tool call.
